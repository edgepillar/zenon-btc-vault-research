Vault design

A proof can release a spend. The signature fixes where it goes.

The prototype targets one Bitcoin output and one recipient. Its mock checks the withdrawal record and releases a previously signed transaction. Bitcoin Core checks the signature, payout, and timelock rules on regtest.

Withdrawal evidence

A Momentum commitment can establish that a block is included under a supplied header. A production exit also needs proof that the withdrawal was accepted under the intended rules and belongs to the history the verifier treats as final. The experimental Zeno state-root path helps establish state inclusion under a verified header.

Setup and refund

A completed pre-signed transaction is already a spending capability. Deleting signing keys leaves existing copies usable. A future encrypted-release setup needs a credible way to protect that capability and verify the encrypted payload. Release and refund must also remain consistent if either path is delayed.

Cryptography

Where Praxis fits

Praxis is a witness-encryption proposal by David Tse, Nikhil Vanjani, and Psi Vesely. For the paper’s BN254 example, a Groth16 proof accepted for the selected public input can serve as a decryption witness. A vault could use that capability to release a narrowly scoped spending authorization.

Praxis paper estimates at its stated 100-bit target. Proof generation is additional. The paper counts arithmetic operations and excludes point compression, hashing, and memory traffic.

The extraction result relies on the paper’s group and random-oracle models and its Module-SIS assumption. The Groth16 example also depends on honest setup and correct encoding of the verifier. The Lean formalization covers the stated model, with the verifier encoding outside its scope.

Roadmap

What would justify the next step?

  1. Complete the regtest experiment

    Integration pending

    Confirm the exact payout and reject a changed output or repeat spend. Reject an early refund and confirm the refund after maturity. Record the result against a pinned Bitcoin Core release.

  2. Consume a real Zenon test witness

    Research

    Verify a real Zeno state entry on a selected v3 test environment. Pin the network, header, state root, and domain context. Then bind an accepted withdrawal to the Bitcoin output, amount, and recipient.

  3. Define when withdrawal evidence is final

    Open

    Specify the verifier’s rules for producer authorization, fork selection, and irreversible release. Exercise stale and conflicting views and show how refund and release are reconciled.

  4. Evaluate encrypted release

    Open

    Require reproducible encryption and decryption, a checked verifier encoding, and an end-to-end measurement of proof generation, runtime, and ciphertext availability. Compare with BABE or BitVM for the same withdrawal and trust model.

The roadmap covers a fixed-recipient vault. A transferable BTC peg introduces recipient selection, liquidity, and issuance and redemption accounting.
